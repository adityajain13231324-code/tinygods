# Tiny Gods idea bank

First selections: 5 November 2026. Updated: 6 November 2026.

**Status, 9 November 2026: all seventeen selected ideas are implemented on the `new-ideas-implemented` branch.** See `docs/IDEAS-IMPLEMENTATION-2026-11-09.md` for how each one works, the rules chosen where this file left them open, and what was verified. The entries below are the original wording.

This file collects ideas for future discussion. Saving an idea does not authorize implementation. All seventeen entries below are selected ideas, not completed features. Keep future suggestions separate until the player chooses which ones to retain.

## Selected ideas

### 1. A richer match ending

Alongside the winning faction, award titles like "Most Chaotic Villager," "Biggest Betrayal," "Best Escape" and "Worst Aim," with a short replay of each moment.

### 2. Optional chaos challenges

Separate modes such as *Everyone Starts Armed*, *One Villager per Faction*, *Explosive Deliveries* or *Random Powers Every Turn*. These should support quick, ridiculous matches.

### 3. Readable combat personality

A frightened launcher holder fires over their shoulder while escaping. A confident gunner briefly takes aim. A villager with one rocket left visibly checks the launcher. Tiny animations give everyone character.

### 4. Proper cover and destructible props

Villagers duck behind benches, market stalls and walls during gunfights. Rockets can destroy some cover, changing how a fight plays out.

### 5. A wandering troublemaker

A mischievous neutral steals a weapon, spreads a ridiculous rumour or runs through a tense confrontation. Players can protect, recruit or chase them.

### 6. Power combos

Rain followed by Lightning creates a small chain zap. Gust pushes a planted bomb into another position. Keep combos understandable, with clear visual feedback and limited bonus damage.

### 7. Personal rivalries across the match

A villager remembers who scared, injured or betrayed them. They recognise each other with little reactions: "YOU again!" Forgiveness could also happen after healing or a successful peace treaty.

### 8. Environmental chain reactions

Explosions scatter market goods, fire spreads through a small patch of dry grass, and water puts it out. Each reaction should be visible early enough for players to respond.

### 9. Different town layouts

Canal towns with narrow bridges, crowded market streets, scattered farms or a village built around a hill. Each layout changes where confrontations happen.

### 10. More dialogue that reacts to events

Add many more dialogue lines that refer to what is actually happening, making the town feel more immersive. Reactions should fit the speaker, the people involved and the current situation rather than feel like unrelated chatter.

Possible examples: "That was our last rocket!", "You healed me? After everything?", "The armory is empty!" and "They froze my friend!" These are illustrative lines, not a final dialogue list. Variety and sensible timing should keep repeated lines and overlapping bubbles from becoming distracting.

### 11. Conditional orders

"Guard the armory, but retreat to the park if outnumbered." Let players create clever plans that adapt when things go wrong.

### 12. Squad formations

Choose "spread out," "stick together" or "protect the centre." Spreading out helps against rockets; grouping helps villagers support each other.

### 13. Funny, shareable highlight clips

Turn a ridiculous escape, betrayal or rocket miss into a short clip with the villager's name, a funny caption and the creator watermark, `@adityajain1323`. Let players choose which moment to save. Emphasize the humour and unexpected stories that emerge during a match.

### 14. Blank Rounds card

Sabotage a rival's first few gunshots so they fire harmless confetti. The gunner looks at their weapon in disbelief before normal ammunition resumes. The exact number of affected shots and targeting rules remain to be decided.

### 15. Free Cake! card

Put up an irresistible invitation at a location. Some idle or curious villagers investigate, creating opportunities for distraction, recruitment or an ambush.

### 16. Weapons dropped on death

An armed villager has a 30% chance to drop their weapon when they die. Pickup behavior, ammunition retained, eligible weapon types and how long the dropped weapon stays available remain to be decided.

### 17. Leader card costing 3 power

Choose one villager from your own faction to become the leader. They gain twice their maximum health, fully replenish their current health, and gain Strength as well. The card costs 3 power and can only be used once per match. A small mouse-hover note explains the once-per-match limit.

The Strength bonus amount and duration, whether the once-per-match limit is per player or global, and where this power-costing card belongs in the UI remain to be decided. This is a saved concept, not an implemented upgrade.

## Decisions still open

Implementation order, exact rules, balance, art, performance costs and multiplayer behavior are not decided yet. No gameplay changes are part of this idea-gathering task.
